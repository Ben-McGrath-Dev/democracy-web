# DEMOCRACY

## Constitution & Rules

### A WhatsApp/Web Political Simulation Game

**Version 2**

---

# PART I — THE CONSTITUTION

## 1. Purpose and Authority [BASE]

Democracy is a political simulation game governed by this Constitution.

The Constitution is the highest authority within the game.

Every player, political party, government, committee, office-holder, administrator and institution is subject to the Constitution.

No person or institution may exercise a power that the Constitution does not give them.

No person is above the Constitution.

---

## 2. Constitutional Hierarchy [BASE]

Rules in Democracy have the following order of authority:

1. Base Constitution;
2. Editable Constitution;
3. Laws;
4. Regulations and administrative rules;
5. Committee and administrative decisions.

A lower-level rule or decision is invalid to the extent that it contradicts a higher-level rule.

Where two rules at the same level conflict, the more specific rule applies.

Where they remain impossible to reconcile, the Actions Committee may issue a constitutional interpretation under Section 48.

---

## 3. Categories of Constitutional Rules [BASE]

Constitutional rules are either:

- **Base Rules**; or
- **Editable Rules**.

A section marked **[BASE]** is part of the Base Constitution.

A section marked **[EDITABLE]** may be amended through the normal constitutional amendment procedure.

If a section is not expressly marked, it is considered **[EDITABLE]**.

---

## 4. Base Constitutional Rules [BASE]

Base Rules cannot be directly amended through the normal amendment procedure.

The following institutions must continue to exist and retain meaningful constitutional functions:

- Host;
- Deputy Host;
- Actions Committee;
- Punishment Committee;
- People’s Actions Committee;
- People’s Punishment Committee;
- elected legislature;
- jury system;
- public elections.

An amendment may not technically preserve the name of an institution while removing all or substantially all of its constitutional function.

The following principles are also Base Rules:

- political power must remain capable of changing through elections;
- the Host must remain politically neutral while exercising Host powers;
- accused players are entitled to fair process;
- no person may decide a case directly concerning themselves;
- laws cannot retroactively punish conduct;
- the Constitution is superior to ordinary laws;
- permanent removal may not be imposed for an ordinary law violation alone;
- no institution possesses unlimited constitutional authority.

---

## 5. Making a Base Rule Editable [BASE]

A Base Rule may become editable only through the following procedure.

A proposal may be initiated by:

- the Host; or
- a petition signed by at least 25% of active players.

The proposal must identify the exact Base Rule that would become editable and explain why.

The Actions Committee must approve the proposal by at least a two-thirds majority of its full non-recused membership.

If the AC approves it, the proposal proceeds to a public vote.

The public vote requires:

- at least 75% of valid votes cast in favour; and
- participation by at least 50% of the electorate for that vote.

If both requirements are satisfied, the specified provision becomes editable.

The provision does not itself change at that point.

A separate constitutional amendment must then be passed if players wish to alter its wording.

---

# PART II — DEFINITIONS AND PLAYERS

## 6. Definitions [EDITABLE]

For this Constitution:

**Active Player**  
A player currently entitled to participate in Democracy and not recorded as resigned, permanently removed or inactive under Section 8.

**Electorate**  
The players eligible to vote in a particular vote when its electorate snapshot is created.

**Electorate Snapshot**  
The official list and number of eligible voters fixed when a vote opens.

**Valid Vote**  
A vote submitted by an eligible voter in accordance with the announced voting procedure.

**Ordinary Majority**  
More than 50% of valid votes cast.

A 50–50 result does not constitute an ordinary majority.

**Legislative Majority**  
More than half of all filled legislative seats.

**Two-Thirds Majority**  
At least two-thirds of the membership or valid votes specified by the applicable rule.

**Full Membership**  
Every currently seated member of a body, excluding vacant seats.

**Decision Membership**  
The full membership eligible to participate in a particular decision after required recusals.

**Quorum**  
The minimum participation necessary for a valid decision.

**Recusal**  
Temporary exclusion from a specific matter because of a conflict of interest.

**Running Government**  
The party, electoral list or coalition currently controlling a legislative majority and therefore entitled to form the government.

**Government Party**  
A party or electoral list participating in the Running Government.

**Opposition**  
Political parties or electoral lists not participating in the Running Government.

**Election**  
A vote used to choose an office-holder, representative or elected body.

**Referendum**  
A public vote on a law, amendment or specified political question.

**Constitutional Violation**  
Conduct violating an obligation imposed directly by the Constitution.

**Ordinary Law Violation**  
Conduct violating a law enacted beneath the Constitution.

**Day**  
A continuous period of 24 hours unless expressly stated otherwise.

---

## 7. Minimum Player Count [EDITABLE]

Democracy requires at least 25 active players for normal operation.

The Host counts as an active player.

If active player numbers fall below 25, the Host may suspend:

- new general elections;
- new constitutional amendments;
- new ordinary legislation;
- new non-essential political procedures.

The following may continue:

- existing cases;
- necessary committee proceedings;
- replacement of essential office-holders;
- maintenance of records;
- emergency administrative action;
- procedures necessary to restore normal operation.

Normal political activity resumes automatically when at least 25 active players are again available.

---

## 8. Player Status [EDITABLE]

A player may have one of the following statuses:

- Active;
- Temporarily Inactive;
- Resigned;
- Permanently Removed.

### 8.1 Voluntary Resignation

A player may resign by clearly notifying the Host.

Resignation takes effect when recorded by the Host.

### 8.2 Temporary Inactivity

A player may ask to be recorded as temporarily inactive.

The Host may also ask a player to confirm their status where that player has had no game participation for at least 14 days.

A player cannot be declared inactive merely because of their political opinions or voting behaviour.

### 8.3 Automatic Inactivity

A player who fails to respond to a formal activity-status request within 7 days may be recorded as temporarily inactive.

The request must be reasonably delivered to the player.

### 8.4 Returning From Inactivity

A temporarily inactive player may return to Active status by notifying the Host.

They do not gain eligibility for a vote whose electorate snapshot has already been taken.

### 8.5 Rejoining After Resignation

A resigned player may apply to rejoin.

Unless prohibited by another constitutional rule, they return as a new active player.

### 8.6 Permanent Removal

A permanently removed player cannot return unless a constitutional procedure expressly permits it.

---

## 9. Electorate Snapshot [BASE]

Eligibility for a particular public vote is determined when voting opens.

The Host must publish:

- number of eligible voters;
- eligibility cutoff time;
- opening time;
- closing time.

Players joining or returning from inactivity after voting opens do not vote in that particular vote.

Players who become inactive after voting opens remain part of that electorate unless they resign or become legally ineligible before casting a vote.

Turnout requirements are calculated from the electorate snapshot, not from later changes in player numbers.

---

# PART III — FUNDAMENTAL POLITICAL RIGHTS

## 10. Political Rights [BASE]

Every eligible player has the following rights.

### 10.1 Right to Vote

Eligible players may vote in elections and referendums in which they are included in the electorate.

### 10.2 Right to Political Participation

Players may:

- form political parties and electoral lists;
- join political parties;
- campaign;
- petition;
- participate in political debate;
- support or oppose proposals.

### 10.3 Right to Stand for Office

Eligible players may stand for elected positions subject to neutral eligibility requirements established by this Constitution.

### 10.4 Political Expression

Players may criticise:

- governments;
- politicians;
- political parties;
- institutions;
- laws;
- proposed laws;
- constitutional provisions;
- game decisions.

This right remains subject to lawful rules concerning serious harassment, threats, impersonation and similar misconduct.

### 10.5 Right to Fair Process

A player accused of wrongdoing has the right to:

- know the exact allegation;
- know the rule allegedly violated;
- see the evidence relied upon, except information legitimately withheld for safety or privacy;
- submit evidence;
- provide a response;
- receive reasonable time to respond;
- have conflicts of interest addressed;
- receive a reasoned decision.

### 10.6 Presumption of Innocence

A player accused of an ordinary law violation is treated as not guilty unless a guilty finding survives the constitutional PAC and jury process.

---

# PART IV — THE HOST

## 11. The Host [BASE]

The Host is the principal neutral administrator of Democracy.

The Host is not the government and does not possess supreme political authority.

The Host:

- administers the game;
- maintains official records;
- administers public votes;
- certifies results;
- enforces procedural deadlines;
- maintains the official Constitution and laws;
- performs administrative functions;
- appoints juries where required;
- maintains the electorate register;
- ensures approved procedures are followed.

---

## 12. Host Neutrality [BASE]

While exercising Host powers, the Host must remain politically neutral.

The Host must not:

- campaign for a political party while acting as Host;
- advocate for or against a proposal while using Host authority;
- manipulate procedures for political advantage;
- selectively alter deadlines;
- selectively enforce rules;
- use confidential voting information politically;
- suppress a lawful result because the Host dislikes it;
- use administrative powers to favour or disadvantage a political participant.

The Host may vote as an ordinary player unless prohibited by another rule.

The Host must clearly distinguish personal voting from official Host announcements.

---

## 13. Host Eligibility [EDITABLE]

A Host candidate must:

- be an active player;
- agree to remain neutral while acting as Host;
- not currently lead a political party;
- not simultaneously serve as Prime Minister;
- not simultaneously serve on the AC, PC, PAC or PPC.

A serving Host may seek re-election.

---

## 14. Host Election and Term [EDITABLE]

The Host is elected by ranked-choice voting.

The normal Host term is **56 days**.

A new Host election begins sufficiently early for the successor to take office when the current term expires.

If no candidate receives more than 50% of first-choice votes, ranked transfers are conducted according to Section 35.

The Host election must not be administered by a Host who is standing as a candidate.

In that situation the Deputy Host or an AC-appointed Returning Officer administers the election.

---

# PART V — DEPUTY HOST AND CONTINUITY

## 15. Deputy Host [BASE]

Democracy has a Deputy Host.

The Deputy Host is a neutral administrative office.

The Deputy Host does not obtain additional political power merely by holding the office.

The Deputy Host:

- assists with administration;
- acts where the Host is temporarily unavailable;
- acts where the Host is constitutionally recused;
- administers a proceeding involving the Host where appropriate;
- preserves continuity if the Host resigns or is removed.

The Deputy Host is subject to the same neutrality rules as the Host while exercising administrative powers.

---

## 16. Deputy Host Election and Term [EDITABLE]

The Deputy Host is elected separately by ranked-choice voting.

The normal term is 56 days.

The Deputy Host may not simultaneously:

- lead a political party;
- serve as Prime Minister;
- serve on the AC, PC, PAC or PPC.

---

## 17. Acting Host [BASE]

The Deputy Host becomes Acting Host when:

- the Host resigns;
- the Host is temporarily unavailable for more than 24 hours during a time-sensitive procedure;
- the Host is suspended;
- the Host is constitutionally recused;
- the Host is permanently removed.

An Acting Host exercises only the powers necessary to administer the game.

If the Host position becomes permanently vacant, a new Host election must begin within 7 days.

If both Host and Deputy Host are unavailable or conflicted, the AC appoints a Temporary Neutral Administrator by ordinary majority of its full non-recused membership.

---

# PART VI — THE LEGISLATURE

## 18. Legislature [BASE]

Democracy has an elected legislature.

The legislature:

- represents voters;
- passes ordinary laws;
- amends ordinary laws;
- repeals ordinary laws;
- determines whether a government has legislative confidence;
- debates government policy;
- holds ministers politically accountable;
- performs other powers granted by this Constitution.

The legislature does not possess authority to override the Constitution.

---

## 19. Legislature Size [EDITABLE]

Legislature size is determined by the number of active players when a general election opens.

| Active Players | Legislative Seats |
|---|---:|
| 25–39 | 7 |
| 40–59 | 10 |
| 60–99 | 15 |
| 100+ | 20 |

The legislature keeps that size until the next general election.

---

## 20. Legislative Term [EDITABLE]

The normal legislative term is **28 days**.

The next general election must be scheduled so a new legislature can take office when the term ends.

An early election may occur where this Constitution requires one.

---

## 21. Electoral Lists and Candidates [EDITABLE]

Legislative elections are contested by Electoral Lists.

An Electoral List may be:

- a registered political party; or
- an independent group of candidates registered for that election.

Before voting opens, each list must provide an ordered list of legislative candidates.

A player may appear on only one legislative candidate list in the same election.

A candidate must consent to their nomination.

---

## 22. Allocation of Legislative Seats [EDITABLE]

A list must receive at least 10% of valid votes cast to qualify for seats.

Votes for lists below the threshold are excluded from proportional allocation.

Seats are distributed among qualifying lists using the largest remainder method.

The allocation process is:

1. Determine each qualifying list's share of the qualifying vote.
2. Multiply that share by the number of legislative seats.
3. Allocate the whole-number part of each result.
4. Allocate remaining seats according to the largest decimal remainders.
5. If two lists have an identical remainder, the list with the higher raw valid vote receives priority.
6. If raw vote totals are also tied, a public tie-breaking vote is held.

All legislative seats must be allocated.

---

## 23. Legislative Members [EDITABLE]

Legislative seats are occupied according to each Electoral List's published candidate order.

The first candidate takes the first seat, the second candidate takes the second seat, and so on.

A person may occupy only one legislative seat.

A legislator may also hold a ministerial office but may not simultaneously serve as:

- Host;
- Deputy Host;
- AC member;
- PC member;
- PAC member;
- PPC member.

---

## 24. Legislative Vacancies [EDITABLE]

Where a legislator:

- resigns their legislative seat;
- becomes permanently ineligible;
- is permanently removed from Democracy;
- is inactive for more than 7 consecutive days without approved leave,

their seat becomes vacant.

The next eligible person on that Electoral List's candidate list takes the seat.

If no eligible candidate remains, the seat remains vacant until the next election unless a by-election procedure is authorised.

A legislative majority is calculated using filled seats.

---

## 25. Party Membership and Seats [EDITABLE]

A legislative seat belongs to the Electoral List that won it.

If a legislator voluntarily leaves the political party or Electoral List through which they received their seat, they lose that legislative seat.

The next eligible candidate on the list replaces them.

This rule does not prohibit legislators from disagreeing with their party or voting against party instructions.

---

# PART VII — GOVERNMENT

## 26. Government Formation [EDITABLE]

The party, list or coalition controlling a legislative majority forms the Running Government.

Following a general election:

1. The Host certifies the result.
2. A list with a legislative majority may immediately attempt to form a government.
3. If no list has a majority, the list with the largest number of seats receives the first opportunity to form a coalition.
4. It has 72 hours to demonstrate a legislative majority.
5. If it fails, any other combination of lists may attempt to form a government during a second 72-hour period.
6. If no government can demonstrate a legislative majority after that period, a new general election is held.

A coalition must publicly identify:

- participating parties or lists;
- the proposed Prime Minister;
- confirmation that the coalition controls a legislative majority.

---

## 27. Prime Minister [EDITABLE]

The Running Government selects the Prime Minister.

The Prime Minister:

- leads the government;
- coordinates government policy;
- introduces government legislation;
- represents the government politically;
- appoints and dismisses ministers.

The Prime Minister may not simultaneously serve as Host or Deputy Host.

---

## 28. Ministers [EDITABLE]

The Prime Minister may appoint up to five ministers.

Possible portfolios include:

- Finance;
- Justice;
- Foreign Affairs;
- Internal Affairs;
- Communications.

A minister has only those powers granted by:

- the Constitution;
- a valid law;
- a valid administrative rule.

The Prime Minister may dismiss a minister at any time.

---

## 29. Confidence and No Confidence [EDITABLE]

The government must maintain a legislative majority.

### 29.1 Confidence Vote

The Prime Minister may request a vote of confidence.

The government retains confidence if a legislative majority votes in favour.

### 29.2 Constructive No-Confidence Vote

A no-confidence motion may be proposed by:

- at least one-third of filled legislative seats; or
- a public petition signed by at least 20% of active players.

The motion must identify a proposed replacement Prime Minister or replacement governing coalition.

The motion passes only if supported by a legislative majority.

If it passes, the replacement government takes office once its legislative majority is verified.

### 29.3 Loss of Majority Without a Replacement

If the government loses its majority without a successful constructive no-confidence motion:

1. the Host records the loss of majority;
2. the existing government becomes a caretaker government;
3. political parties have 72 hours to demonstrate an alternative legislative majority;
4. if an alternative succeeds, it forms the new government;
5. otherwise, a new general election is held.

A caretaker government may perform necessary administration but should not make major new policy decisions except where urgently necessary.

---

# PART VIII — ORDINARY LAWS

## 30. Nature of Laws [BASE]

Laws govern player behaviour and other matters authorised by the Constitution.

A law may not:

- contradict the Constitution;
- retroactively punish conduct;
- be so vague that players cannot reasonably understand it;
- require illegal conduct;
- remove constitutional rights;
- grant an institution unlimited power;
- create permanent removal as punishment for an ordinary law violation.

---

## 31. Introducing Legislation [EDITABLE]

A law may be introduced by:

- the Running Government;
- any Electoral List holding at least one legislative seat;
- a petition signed by at least 10% of active players.

Every proposal must contain:

- proposal ID;
- proposer;
- complete proposed wording;
- reason for the proposal;
- laws affected;
- proposed commencement date, if different from the default.

---

## 32. Legislative Procedure [EDITABLE]

A normal bill follows this procedure:

1. Publication.
2. At least 24 hours of public discussion.
3. Final wording is frozen.
4. Legislature votes.
5. The proposal passes if supported by a legislative majority.
6. The Host certifies the result.

A bill cannot be substantively edited after voting opens.

A substantive change requires a new version and a new vote.

---

## 33. Public Referendum on a Law [EDITABLE]

A law passed by the legislature may be subjected to a public referendum if, within 48 hours of legislative passage:

- at least 20% of active players sign a referendum petition; or
- a legislative majority voluntarily refers it to the public.

Where a valid referendum request is made, the law does not take effect until the referendum concludes.

The referendum passes if:

- more than 50% of valid votes cast support the law; and
- at least 20% of the electorate participates.

If the referendum rejects the law, the law does not take effect.

---

## 34. Citizens' Initiative [EDITABLE]

A proposal supported by at least 20% of active players may be taken directly to a public referendum even if the legislature refuses to pass it.

The normal constitutional restrictions on laws still apply.

The referendum requires:

- more than 50% approval; and
- at least 20% turnout.

The same procedure may be used to amend or repeal an ordinary law.

---

# PART IX — ELECTIONS AND VOTING

## 35. Election Systems [EDITABLE]

Different elections use different voting systems.

### 35.1 Legislature

Legislative elections use proportional representation under Section 22.

### 35.2 Single-Person Offices

Host and Deputy Host elections use ranked-choice voting.

Voters rank candidates.

If a candidate receives more than 50% of active ballots, that candidate wins.

Otherwise:

1. the candidate with the fewest votes is eliminated;
2. ballots transfer to each voter's next available preference;
3. the process repeats;
4. the first candidate to exceed 50% wins.

### 35.3 Committee Elections

Committee elections use approval voting.

A voter may approve up to the number of available seats.

Candidates with the highest approval totals are elected.

Ties affecting who receives a seat are resolved by a tie-breaking vote.

---

## 36. Election Duration [BASE]

An ordinary public election must remain open for at least 48 hours.

A constitutional amendment vote must remain open for at least 48 hours.

An ordinary public referendum must remain open for at least 24 hours.

The announced closing time cannot be changed for political advantage.

---

## 37. WhatsApp Voting Procedure [BASE]

Before voting begins, the administrator must publish:

- vote title;
- proposal or office being voted on;
- electorate size;
- voting method;
- whether voting is secret or public;
- opening time;
- closing time;
- options or candidates;
- turnout requirement;
- passage threshold;
- instructions for submitting a ballot.

### 37.1 Secret Votes

The following are secret unless the Constitution expressly provides otherwise:

- general elections;
- Host elections;
- Deputy Host elections;
- public referendums;
- constitutional amendment votes;
- removal votes.

Secret votes may be submitted through:

- direct message to the authorised Returning Officer;
- an approved anonymous voting system;
- another method publicly approved before voting begins.

### 37.2 Public Votes

Legislative votes and most committee votes may be public.

A WhatsApp poll may be used where:

- voter identity is permitted to be public;
- the poll settings support the constitutional voting method;
- the administrator preserves the result.

### 37.3 Changing a Ballot

Where the voting system permits ballot changes, a voter may change their ballot before the deadline.

Only the final valid ballot counts.

### 37.4 Late Votes

A ballot received after the published deadline is invalid unless the entire vote has been formally extended under a constitutional procedure.

### 37.5 Duplicate Votes

If a voter submits multiple ballots, only the latest valid ballot submitted before the deadline counts.

### 37.6 Abstentions

An abstention does not count as a vote in favour or against.

Where turnout is based on participation, a formally recorded abstention counts toward turnout unless another rule states otherwise.

### 37.7 Invalid Votes

A vote may be invalid if:

- cast by an ineligible person;
- submitted after the deadline;
- impossible to interpret;
- submitted using an unauthorised method;
- demonstrably fraudulent.

A vote must not be invalidated merely because the administrator dislikes it.

---

## 38. Technical Failure [EDITABLE]

If WhatsApp or the authorised voting system experiences a significant technical failure, the Host may temporarily pause the vote.

The pause must:

- be publicly announced;
- apply equally to all participants;
- state the reason;
- last no longer than necessary.

The closing deadline must be extended by at least the duration of the interruption.

A technical failure may not be used to change a political result.

---

## 39. Election Results [BASE]

The administrator must publish:

- electorate size;
- votes cast;
- turnout;
- valid votes;
- invalid votes;
- candidate or option totals;
- transfers where applicable;
- seat allocation where applicable;
- final result.

Secret individual ballots must not be publicly linked to voters.

---

# PART X — ELECTORAL FRAUD

## 40. Suspected Election Fraud [BASE]

Suspected fraud does not automatically destroy an election.

Where the Host has a genuine and reasonable basis to suspect significant fraud, the Host may temporarily suspend voting or certification.

The Host must immediately:

1. preserve available voting records;
2. publish that the vote has been suspended;
3. describe the general reason without unnecessarily exposing confidential information;
4. refer the matter to the AC.

---

## 41. AC Fraud Review [BASE]

The AC must review the suspension as quickly as reasonably possible.

The AC may determine that:

- no material fraud occurred and voting should resume;
- a correctable problem occurred;
- specified invalid votes should be excluded;
- part of the process must be repeated;
- the entire election is irreparably compromised and must be rerun.

An entire election should be voided only where the integrity of the result cannot reasonably be restored.

If the AC finds that the Host used fraud allegations as a political pretext, the matter is referred to the PC.

---

# PART XI — ACTIONS COMMITTEE

## 42. Actions Committee [BASE]

The Actions Committee is an elected constitutional oversight body.

The AC:

- reviews alleged constitutional violations;
- reviews Host neutrality disputes;
- reviews election administration;
- reviews emergency administrative actions;
- reviews suspected election fraud;
- determines whether laws are constitutional;
- interprets ambiguous constitutional provisions;
- exercises other constitutional powers expressly granted to it.

The AC determines constitutional compliance.

It does not determine punishment.

Where the AC finds a constitutional violation, the matter is referred to the PC if punishment is appropriate.

---

# PART XII — PUNISHMENT COMMITTEE

## 43. Punishment Committee [BASE]

The Punishment Committee determines punishment after the AC has found a constitutional violation.

The PC may not reverse the AC's constitutional finding merely because it disagrees with it.

Ordinary constitutional punishments require an ordinary majority of the full Decision Membership.

---

## 44. Constitutional Punishments [BASE]

Permitted constitutional punishments include:

### Warning

Formal notice that constitutional conduct was improper.

### Public Censure

A public statement explaining the violation.

A censure may be humorous where appropriate but must not require threats, discriminatory abuse, degrading material or unlawful conduct.

### Corrective Action

An order to correct an administrative or procedural mistake where correction remains possible.

### Temporary Suspension

Temporary loss of specified game powers or privileges for a defined period.

### Removal From Office

Removal from a particular elected or administrative position.

### Permanent Removal From Democracy

Permanent removal from the game.

Permanent removal requires unanimous approval of every non-recused PC member participating in a constitutionally valid panel.

If unanimity is not obtained, permanent removal fails and another permitted punishment may be considered.

Punishments must be:

- proportionate;
- specific;
- lawful;
- related to the violation.

---

# PART XIII — PEOPLE'S ACTIONS COMMITTEE

## 45. People's Actions Committee [BASE]

The People's Actions Committee investigates alleged violations of ordinary laws.

The PAC does not impose punishment.

The PAC operates through case panels.

---

## 46. PAC Case Panels [EDITABLE]

A normal case is assigned to a panel of three PAC members.

Panel members must:

- have no disqualifying conflict;
- receive the relevant evidence;
- allow the accused to respond.

A PAC panel decides whether the alleged violation is proven.

The possible findings are:

- Guilty;
- Not Guilty.

A finding of Guilty requires an ordinary majority of the three-person panel.

---

## 47. Standard of Proof [BASE]

A PAC panel may find a player Guilty only where the available evidence makes it more likely than not that the alleged violation occurred.

Suspicion alone is insufficient.

The burden of establishing the violation rests on the allegation, not on the accused player proving innocence.

---

# PART XIV — CONSTITUTIONAL INTERPRETATION

## 48. Constitutional Interpretation [BASE]

Where constitutional wording is genuinely ambiguous, the AC may issue an interpretation.

An interpretation must:

- identify the disputed wording;
- explain the interpretation adopted;
- rely on the constitutional text and structure;
- be publicly recorded.

The AC may clarify ambiguity.

It may not use interpretation to effectively rewrite clear constitutional wording.

An interpretation remains authoritative unless:

- changed through a valid constitutional amendment;
- replaced by a later valid interpretation addressing a genuine conflict;
- found procedurally invalid under an expressly authorised review procedure.

---

# PART XV — ORDINARY LAW CASES

## 49. Opening a Case [BASE]

An allegation must identify:

- accused player;
- law allegedly violated;
- relevant conduct;
- available evidence.

The accused must receive notice of the allegation.

---

## 50. Case Procedure [BASE]

A normal case follows this process.

### Step 1 — Allegation

A valid allegation is submitted.

### Step 2 — PAC Panel

A three-person PAC panel investigates.

The accused receives a reasonable opportunity to respond.

The panel issues:

- Guilty; or
- Not Guilty.

### Step 3 — Jury Review

Every Guilty finding is automatically reviewed by a jury.

A Not Guilty finding ends the case unless a review is validly requested under Section 52.

### Step 4 — Guilty Finding Upheld

If a jury upholds a Guilty finding, the matter proceeds to the PPC.

### Step 5 — Guilty Finding Rejected

If a jury rejects a Guilty finding, the accused is considered Not Guilty and the case ends.

---

## 51. Jury [BASE]

A normal jury consists of five eligible players.

Jurors must not:

- be the accused;
- be the complainant;
- have participated in the PAC panel;
- be directly involved in the alleged incident;
- have a substantial conflict of interest.

The jury votes on whether the PAC finding should be upheld.

An ordinary majority is required.

---

## 52. Review of a Not Guilty PAC Finding [EDITABLE]

A Not Guilty PAC finding may be reviewed only if:

- significant evidence was ignored;
- new material evidence has appeared;
- a serious procedural error occurred;
- a material conflict of interest affected the original panel.

The request must identify the specific ground.

If review is authorised, a completely new PAC panel hears the case.

No member of the original panel may sit on the replacement panel.

A new jury must review any resulting Guilty finding.

If the second PAC panel finds Not Guilty, the case ends.

---

## 53. Jury Deadlock [EDITABLE]

If a jury does not reach a majority by its deadline:

1. the jury receives an additional 24 hours;
2. if no majority is reached, the jury is dismissed;
3. a new jury is selected.

If the second jury also fails to reach a majority, the accused is considered Not Guilty and the case ends.

---

## 54. Finality and Repeat Cases [BASE]

Once a player has received a constitutionally final Not Guilty result, substantially the same allegation based on the same conduct may not be repeatedly brought against them.

A new case may be permitted only where genuinely new and material evidence exists that was not reasonably available during the original case.

The AC determines whether this exception is satisfied if disputed.

---

# PART XVI — PEOPLE'S PUNISHMENT COMMITTEE

## 55. People's Punishment Committee [BASE]

The PPC determines punishment only after a Guilty finding has been upheld through the required jury process.

The PPC:

- does not determine guilt;
- does not overturn a jury decision;
- may impose only punishments authorised by the applicable law or Constitution.

---

## 56. Ordinary Law Punishments [BASE]

Permitted punishment types may include:

- warning;
- public statement;
- temporary restriction of specified political privileges;
- temporary suspension from specified game activities;
- another non-permanent penalty expressly authorised by law.

A punishment must be:

- proportionate;
- clearly defined;
- connected to the violation;
- lawful.

Permanent removal from Democracy cannot be imposed solely as punishment for an ordinary law violation.

---

## 57. Overlapping Constitutional and Law Cases [EDITABLE]

The same conduct may potentially violate both:

- an ordinary law; and
- the Constitution.

The two procedures may operate separately where each addresses a genuinely separate rule.

However, punishment bodies must consider any punishment already imposed for the same conduct and avoid unjustified duplicate punishment for the same harm.

---

# PART XVII — COMMITTEES

## 58. Committee Sizes [EDITABLE]

Committee size is determined at the time of the relevant committee election.

| Active Players | AC | PC | PAC | PPC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25–39 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 40–59 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 60–99 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| 100+ | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 |

No permanent committee may have fewer than 3 or more than 10 members.

---

## 59. Committee Terms [EDITABLE]

Committee members normally serve for 56 days.

Committee elections may take place simultaneously.

A player may serve on only one of the four permanent committees at the same time.

---

## 60. Committee Alternates [EDITABLE]

Committee elections also elect alternates.

Where practical:

- committees of 3 elect 1 alternate;
- committees of 5 or more elect 2 alternates.

An alternate takes a permanent vacant seat when a member:

- resigns;
- is permanently removed;
- becomes permanently ineligible;
- is removed for inactivity.

Alternates may also temporarily participate where recusals would otherwise prevent a valid panel.

---

## 61. Committee Chairs [EDITABLE]

Each committee elects one chair by ordinary majority of its full membership.

The chair:

- manages proceedings;
- announces committee votes;
- maintains decision records;
- applies deadlines;
- begins deadlock procedures.

The chair receives no additional vote and has no casting vote.

---

## 62. Committee Voting [EDITABLE]

Unless another section states otherwise, a committee decision requires an ordinary majority of its Decision Membership.

Where a rule expressly requires a majority of full membership, non-voting members continue to count toward the threshold.

Committee members normally have 72 hours to vote.

---

## 63. Committee Deadlock [EDITABLE]

If the required threshold is not reached because of a tie or non-voting members:

1. an additional 24-hour period is allowed;
2. if the threshold is still not reached, the proposal fails unless another rule provides otherwise.

A new proposal may later be submitted.

---

## 64. Committee Inactivity [EDITABLE]

A committee member may be considered for inactivity removal if they miss:

- two formal voting deadlines within 30 days; or
- one voting deadline and fail to respond to an activity check within 72 hours.

Removal requires a two-thirds majority of the other eligible committee members.

The affected member does not vote on their own removal.

A member may not be removed through this process if doing so would leave the committee below its constitutional minimum unless an alternate immediately takes the seat.

---

# PART XVIII — CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

## 65. Mandatory Recusal [BASE]

No person may decide a case directly concerning themselves.

A committee member or juror must recuse where:

- they are the accused;
- they are the complainant;
- they are directly involved in the disputed event;
- they have a direct personal interest in the outcome;
- another substantial conflict makes impartial participation unreasonable.

---

## 66. Disputed Recusal [EDITABLE]

A member may voluntarily recuse.

A participant may also request that a member be recused.

Where a recusal request is disputed:

- the affected member may explain their position;
- the remaining eligible members decide;
- the affected member does not vote on the recusal question.

---

## 67. Insufficient Eligible Committee Members [BASE]

If recusals leave fewer than three eligible committee members:

1. eligible committee alternates are used first;
2. if insufficient alternates exist, temporary panel members are randomly selected from eligible active players;
3. temporary members must satisfy the same conflict rules;
4. temporary membership lasts only for that matter.

The Host administers the random selection unless the Host is conflicted.

If the Host is conflicted, the Deputy Host performs the selection.

If both are conflicted, an uninvolved AC-appointed administrator performs it.

---

# PART XIX — POLITICAL PARTIES

## 68. Political Parties [BASE]

Any active player may apply to create a political party.

The application must identify:

- party name;
- leader or leadership structure;
- basic political programme;
- internal method for changing leadership.

A party may not deliberately impersonate another party or official institution.

---

## 69. Party Rights [BASE]

Political parties may:

- campaign;
- contest elections;
- nominate candidates;
- introduce legislation where constitutionally eligible;
- support petitions;
- propose constitutional amendments where eligible;
- participate in political debate;
- form coalitions.

Government and opposition parties possess equal basic constitutional rights to political participation.

---

# PART XX — CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS

## 70. Amendment Proposals [BASE]

An amendment to an editable constitutional provision may be proposed by:

- the Running Government;
- an Electoral List holding at least one legislative seat;
- a petition signed by at least 10% of active players.

The amendment must identify:

- proposal ID;
- existing constitutional wording;
- proposed replacement wording;
- reason for the change;
- affected sections.

---

## 71. Amendment Procedure [BASE]

A proposed amendment must:

1. be published;
2. remain open for discussion for at least 48 hours;
3. have its final wording frozen before voting;
4. be submitted to a public vote.

The amendment passes only if:

- at least 66% of valid votes cast support it; and
- at least 25% of the electorate participates.

A tied result fails.

---

## 72. Commencement of Amendments [EDITABLE]

Unless an amendment specifies a later date, it takes effect when the Host officially certifies the result.

An amendment cannot retroactively invalidate conduct that was constitutionally lawful before the amendment took effect.

---

# PART XXI — HOST REMOVAL AND MISCONDUCT

## 73. Host Removal Vote [BASE]

A Host removal vote may be initiated by:

- a petition signed by at least 20% of active players; or
- a political party or Electoral List supported by at least 20% of active players.

The Host does not administer their own removal vote.

The Deputy Host administers it unless conflicted.

Otherwise, the AC appoints a neutral Returning Officer.

The Host is removed if:

- at least 90% of valid votes cast support removal; and
- at least 50% of the electorate participates.

The Host may vote as an ordinary voter.

---

## 74. Host Misconduct [BASE]

An allegation that the Host violated the Constitution is submitted to the AC.

The process is:

1. allegation submitted;
2. Host informed;
3. Host given opportunity to respond;
4. conflicted AC members recuse;
5. AC determines whether a constitutional violation occurred;
6. if a violation occurred and punishment is appropriate, matter goes to PC.

The Host does not participate administratively in their own case where doing so would create a conflict.

---

# PART XXII — CHALLENGING LAWS

## 75. Constitutional Challenge to a Law [BASE]

Any active player may challenge a law on constitutional grounds.

The challenge must identify:

- the law;
- the constitutional provision allegedly violated;
- the reason for the challenge.

The AC decides the challenge.

---

## 76. Effect of Constitutional Challenge [EDITABLE]

The AC may temporarily suspend enforcement of a challenged law where continued enforcement could cause significant unfairness before the challenge is resolved.

After review, the AC may:

- uphold the law;
- invalidate the unconstitutional portion;
- invalidate the entire law where the invalid portion cannot reasonably be separated.

The AC must publish its constitutional reasoning.

---

# PART XXIII — APPEALS AND REVIEW

## 77. Limited Review [BASE]

A decision may be reviewed only through a procedure authorised by this Constitution.

Recognised grounds include:

- material procedural error;
- constitutional violation;
- material conflict of interest;
- genuinely new evidence where applicable;
- another expressly authorised ground.

Disagreement with the outcome alone is not a ground for review.

---

## 78. Election Review [BASE]

A player or political participant may challenge an election procedure before the AC.

The challenge must identify a specific alleged constitutional or procedural violation.

The AC may:

- uphold the election procedure;
- order a correctable procedural remedy;
- invalidate a specific administrative action;
- order part of the election repeated;
- order a complete rerun where necessary.

The AC may not change an election result merely because it dislikes the political outcome.

---

## 79. Finality of Committee Decisions [EDITABLE]

Committee decisions are final unless:

- another constitutional section expressly authorises review;
- the committee identifies and corrects its own procedural error before final certification.

No institution possesses a general power to replace another institution's lawful judgment merely because it disagrees with it.

---

# PART XXIV — EMERGENCY ADMINISTRATION

## 80. Emergency Administrative Powers [BASE]

The Host may take temporary administrative action where necessary to prevent Democracy from becoming impossible to operate.

Emergency action must:

- be politically neutral;
- be publicly recorded;
- last no longer than necessary;
- not amend the Constitution;
- not enact or repeal a law;
- not determine guilt;
- not impose punishment;
- not alter a political result.

The AC may review emergency action.

Emergency powers cannot be used to bypass an ordinary constitutional procedure merely because that procedure is inconvenient.

---

# PART XXV — MIGRATION AND NEW PLAYERS

## 81. New Players [EDITABLE]

A player joining after the first general election has been scheduled is considered a new entrant to Democracy.

New entrants possess the same basic constitutional rights as other players.

They may:

- join political parties;
- create political parties;
- participate in debate;
- stand for future offices;
- serve on committees when eligible;
- vote in future votes for which they are included in the electorate snapshot.

No automatic political disadvantage arises merely from joining later.

---

# PART XXVI — OFFICIAL RECORDS

## 82. Official Record [BASE]

The Host must maintain an official record containing:

- current Constitution;
- amendment history;
- current laws;
- law history;
- political parties;
- electoral lists;
- election results;
- legislature membership;
- government membership;
- committee membership;
- active cases;
- constitutional decisions;
- punishments;
- electorate statistics where required.

Official records may not be secretly altered.

---

## 83. Proposal Identification [EDITABLE]

Formal proposals must receive unique IDs.

Recommended formats are:

- LAW-001;
- AMD-001;
- REF-001;
- ELEC-001;
- CASE-001;
- AC-001;
- PC-001.

Numbers must not be reused.

---

## 84. Version Freeze [BASE]

Once formal voting begins, the wording being voted upon is frozen.

Any substantive wording change requires:

- closure of the existing vote;
- publication of the revised version;
- completion of any required discussion period;
- a new vote.

Minor typographical corrections that cannot reasonably change meaning may be recorded without restarting a vote.

---

## 85. Constitutional Changelog [BASE]

Every constitutional amendment must record:

- amendment number;
- date;
- previous wording;
- new wording;
- electorate size;
- votes cast;
- vote result;
- effective date.

Historical versions must be preserved where reasonably possible.

---

# PART XXVII — STARTING LAWS

## 86. Starting Law 1 — No Fraud [EDITABLE]

Players must not deliberately falsify:

- votes;
- election records;
- committee records;
- case evidence;
- official Democracy records.

---

## 87. Starting Law 2 — No Impersonation [EDITABLE]

Players must not deliberately impersonate:

- the Host;
- Deputy Host;
- another player;
- a committee;
- the government;
- another official institution.

Satire or parody that a reasonable participant would understand is not impersonation.

---

## 88. Starting Law 3 — No Vote Interference [EDITABLE]

Players must not deliberately prevent or materially interfere with another eligible player's legitimate ability to vote.

Political persuasion is not vote interference.

---

## 89. Starting Law 4 — No Serious Harassment [EDITABLE]

Players must not use Democracy as a means of serious harassment, threats or targeted abuse.

Ordinary political criticism, disagreement, satire and campaigning do not by themselves constitute serious harassment.

---

## 90. Starting Law 5 — No Retaliation [EDITABLE]

A player must not threaten or punish another player merely for exercising a constitutional political right.

This does not prevent lawful political criticism or constitutionally authorised consequences for actual misconduct.

---

# PART XXVIII — STARTING GAME CONFIGURATION

## 91. First Election [EDITABLE]

The first general election begins once:

- there are at least 25 active players;
- political party registration has been opened;
- candidate lists have been published;
- election rules have been announced.

The first election determines:

- legislature;
- potential governing parties or lists;
- subsequent government formation.

The first general election uses proportional representation.

It does **not** use ranked-choice voting.

---

## 92. First Host and Deputy Host [EDITABLE]

Before or alongside the first general election, players elect:

- Host;
- Deputy Host.

If an original game organiser temporarily performs Host functions before the first Host election, those powers are administrative only.

---

## 93. First Committee Elections [EDITABLE]

The first committee elections elect:

- AC;
- PC;
- PAC;
- PPC.

Committee size is determined by Section 58.

Committee elections may occur alongside the first general election.

---

# PART XXIX — TIES

## 94. Tie Rules [EDITABLE]

Unless another rule provides otherwise:

- a tied yes/no vote fails;
- a tied legislative vote fails;
- a tied committee vote fails to reach a majority;
- a tied jury vote is a jury deadlock;
- an election tie affecting the winner or seat allocation requires a tie-breaking vote.

A required tie-breaking vote should begin within 72 hours where reasonably possible.

---

# PART XXX — SEPARATION OF POWERS

## 95. Institutional Roles [BASE]

### People

Elect representatives and office-holders and exercise referendum and petition powers.

### Legislature

Passes ordinary laws and determines government confidence.

### Government

Exercises political leadership while supported by a legislative majority.

### Prime Minister

Leads the government.

### Host

Administers Democracy neutrally.

### Deputy Host

Provides neutral administrative continuity.

### Actions Committee

Determines constitutional compliance and reviews specified administrative actions.

### Punishment Committee

Determines punishment for constitutional violations.

### People's Actions Committee

Determines whether ordinary laws were violated.

### Jury

Reviews guilty PAC findings.

### People's Punishment Committee

Determines punishment after an upheld ordinary-law conviction.

### Constitution

Creates and limits every institution.

No institution possesses all of these powers.

---

# PART XXXI — FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE

## 96. No One Rules Alone [BASE]

The people choose political representatives.

The legislature makes ordinary laws.

The government governs only while it retains constitutional authority.

The Host administers without controlling political outcomes.

The AC checks constitutional compliance.

The PC determines constitutional punishment.

The PAC examines alleged ordinary law violations.

The jury independently reviews guilty findings.

The PPC determines lawful punishment.

Political parties compete.

Players participate.

The Constitution limits everyone.

Political power must remain capable of changing peacefully through the procedures established by this Constitution.

No player, government, party, committee, administrator or Host is above the Constitution.

Together, these institutions form **Democracy**.
